Let his words sink in. Encouraging people to break the law? It sure seems like it.

Illinois does not require documentary proof of citizenship to register; applicants attest under penalty of law that they are citizens. Words most of them can’t read. Just check the box. Helping citizens who lack a birth certificate or naturalization papers obtain those records is ordinary voter-assistance work. Framing it as a citywide program for “residents” so “every Chicagoan can make their voice heard” treats citizenship as an afterthought rather than the threshold condition.

Chicago election officials state the same rule Johnson’s wording blurs: you must be a U.S. citizen, 18 by Election Day, and a precinct resident for 30 days. Non-citizens who register or vote in those elections commit a crime. If the mayor’s goal is only to help eligible citizens get paperwork, the post should have said “citizens,” not “residents.”
