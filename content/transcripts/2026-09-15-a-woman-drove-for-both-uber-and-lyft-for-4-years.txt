A woman drove for both Uber and Lyft for 4 years over 11,000 trips.

She's also a rider who takes 10+ rides a week.She sees both sides of the app every single day.

"Riders don't realize that the price they see is not the price they have to pay. The app has a bidding system, a scheduling trick that locks lower fares, a rating threshold that gets you matched with better drivers, and a pickup spot change that shaves $3–5 off almost every airport ride. I watch passengers overpay on every trip because they press 'confirm' without checking 3 things first."

"I've driven 11,000 trips. I've watched the surge algorithm spike prices 3x for a ride I know should cost $14. I've watched passengers request pickups from inside a surge zone when walking 2 blocks would have dropped the price by $12. I've watched people take Uber every single day without knowing Lyft is $5 cheaper for the same route at the same time and vice versa. I've watched riders with 4.65 ratings wonder why their drivers cancel on them not realizing that drivers see your rating before they accept and anything below 4.7 is a red flag."

"The rideshare system is not a taxi meter. It's a dynamic pricing marketplace where the fare changes every 30 seconds based on demand, supply, location, time of day, and your own rider history. Two people standing on the same block requesting a ride to the same destination at the same time can pay $8 apart. The system is designed so you never know if you got the best price or the worst one. Unless you know how to check."
