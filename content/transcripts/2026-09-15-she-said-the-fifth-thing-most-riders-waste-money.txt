She said the fifth thing most riders waste money on is airport rides — because they request pickup from the terminal instead of walking to the rideshare pickup zone.

At most major airports, requesting a ride from inside the terminal triggers two things: a higher fare and an airport surcharge.

"Airports are premium surge zones almost 24/7. Demand is constant — hundreds of passengers landing every hour. But driver supply inside the airport pickup zone is limited because drivers have to wait in a staging lot, which takes time and reduces their earnings per hour. The combination of high demand and constrained supply means airport rides almost always cost more than the same ride would from a location a quarter mile outside the airport geofence."

She said the airport surcharge is a separate fee on top of the ride cost.

"Uber and Lyft pay airports a per-trip fee for the right to operate on airport property — typically $3–$5 per pickup. That fee is passed directly to you as an airport surcharge on top of the fare. It's a line item on your receipt that most riders never notice. It's not optional. It's not negotiable. It applies to every ride requested from the airport pickup zone."

She said there are two ways to avoid or reduce these costs.

"First, at many airports, the rideshare pickup zone is not at the terminal curb — it's in a parking garage or a designated lot. The walk from baggage claim to the rideshare zone can take 5–10 minutes. During that walk, check both apps for fare estimates. Sometimes waiting 10 minutes before requesting allows the surge to settle — especially during non-peak hours when the post-landing demand wave passes quickly."

"Second, at some airports, walking to a pickup location just outside the airport geofence eliminates the surcharge entirely. This doesn't work at every airport — some have large geofences that extend well beyond the terminal. But at airports where a hotel, gas station, or parking lot sits just beyond the boundary, requesting from there can save you the $3–$5 surcharge plus the inflated in-zone pricing. Check the fare estimate from the terminal and then from a spot across the street. If the difference is $5–$8, the walk is worth it."
