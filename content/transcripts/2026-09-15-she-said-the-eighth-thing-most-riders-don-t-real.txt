She said the eighth thing most riders don't realize is that the pickup location you set affects the price — and most riders set it wrong.

When you open the app and request a ride, the app auto-fills your current GPS location as the pickup point. Most riders accept it without thinking. She said this costs money in multiple ways.

"First, your GPS location might place you in a surge zone when a location 1–2 blocks away is in a non-surge zone. We talked about that. But there's a second cost most people miss: if your GPS drops you in the middle of a residential block, the driver has to circle around, navigate one-way streets, or double back to reach you. The meter is running from the moment the driver accepts the trip — not from the moment you get in the car. Extra driving time to reach you increases the fare."

She said the fix is to manually set your pickup to the nearest major intersection or easily accessible corner.

"Instead of letting the app pin you wherever your GPS says you are, drag the pin to the nearest corner or main street. This makes pickup faster — the driver pulls up, you get in, you go. Less circling. Less waiting. Less meter running before the trip even starts. On a ride where the driver would have spent 3–4 minutes navigating to your exact GPS pin versus pulling up to a corner in 30 seconds, the fare difference is $1–$3."

She said this is especially important in dense cities with one-way streets, dead-end blocks, and construction.

"In Manhattan, San Francisco, downtown Chicago — setting your pickup to a clean intersection versus letting the GPS drop you mid-block can save you $2–$4 per ride in unnecessary circling time. It also makes the driver's job easier, which means they're more likely to rate you well — which keeps your rating high, which gets you better drivers. Everything is connected."
