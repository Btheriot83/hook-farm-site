Higher fuel prices squeeze $UBER $GRAB mainly through its drivers, who pay for gas themselves as independent contractors. The companies does not automatically reimburse fuel, so costs hit driver earnings first and then ripple through supply, pricing, and trip volume.

Fuel is a large share of operating costs. When prices rise sharply, net pay falls unless drivers work longer hours or become more selective.

Fewer available drivers can tighten supply. Uber’s marketplace then uses dynamic pricing (including surge) to rebalance, so riders may see higher fares or longer waits in busy periods.

In some countries, Uber has added a temporary extra fee on trips (and sometimes Uber Eats) that goes 100% to the driver.

However, demand falls when prices rise (studies have found roughly a 0.4% drop in trips for every 1% rise in fuel prices). Competition from Lyft, taxis, public transit, or simply staying home.

Lastly, $GRAB operates in some of the most volatile region in the world. When drivers/delivery person gets paycut due to surge in fuel, and if commuters/consumers felt price raises too quickly, political unrest might happen.
