She said the third thing most riders don't use is the "Schedule a Ride" feature — and it locks in a fare before surge pricing hits.

Both Uber and Lyft let you schedule rides in advance — typically anywhere from 15 minutes to 30 days before your pickup time. Most riders think this is just a convenience feature for airport trips. She said it's actually a pricing tool.

"When you schedule a ride, the app locks in a fare estimate at the time of scheduling — not at the time of pickup. If you schedule your Friday night ride home at 2:00 PM when there's no surge, you lock in the non-surge fare. When 11:30 PM hits and the surge multiplier is 2.5x, your fare is still based on what you locked in earlier. Everyone else requesting at 11:30 PM is paying surge prices. You're not."

She said this doesn't work perfectly every time — the locked fare is an estimate, not a guarantee, and the final fare can adjust slightly based on actual route and traffic. But the savings compared to requesting during peak surge are significant.

"I schedule my rides home from nights out every single time. I schedule airport pickups 24 hours in advance. I schedule weekday morning commutes the night before if I know rush hour will be busy. The scheduled fare is almost always lower than the real-time fare during peak periods. It's not always the absolute floor price — but it consistently beats surge."

She said Uber's "Reserve" feature takes this even further.

"Uber Reserve lets you book a ride up to 90 days in advance and guarantees a driver will be assigned to you. The fare is locked. The driver is committed. There's no surge adjustment at pickup time. It costs a small premium over the base fare — usually $5–$8 — but on a night when surge would add $20–$30, the Reserve premium saves you $12–$22. For airport rides and event nights, it's the single smartest booking option most riders have never tried."
