## Transcript (~30s)

"Alone In the Wilderness" In 1968 at the age of 51, Richard “Dick” Proenneke made a decision that would completely change the rest of his life. After years working as a diesel mechanic, he abandoned his job and the comforts of modern society and traveled to the isolated Twin Lakes region of Alaska. Instead of simply camping there, Proenneke intended to build a permanent home with his own hands.

Using little more than the resources available around him, he began constructing a log cabin from scratch. He felled trees for the walls, collected gravel from the bed of a nearby lake to create a solid foundation and hauled heavy stones from the surrounding wilderness to build the fireplace. Everything had to be done manually, with no modern construction crew coming to help him.

Once the cabin was finished, Proenneke settled into an extraordinary life of isolation. For roughly three decades, he lived almost completely alone in the Alaskan wilderness, relying mainly on hunting, fishing and gathering wild food to survive. He remained there until he was 83, when age finally convinced him to leave. He moved to California and spent his final years living with his brother.

What makes Proenneke's story even more remarkable is that he documented much of his experience himself. Using an 8mm camera, he filmed the construction of his cabin, the surrounding wilderness and the everyday challenges of living completely alone. After his death in 2003, only four years after he had left Alaska, those recordings were assembled into the documentary Alone in the Wilderness, allowing others to see how he had lived.

Proenneke also made sure that his home would survive after him. He left the cabin to the National Park Service, which preserved it as part of America's historical heritage. Today, the remote cabin remains a popular attraction for visitors and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, turning the home of an Alaskan hermit into a lasting piece of history.
