## Transcript (~30s)

A billion-pound support wrap; four to six fewer Reserve days

17/25 On the 14th of November 2025 — nine days after the IOC celebration and eight days before Titan Storm — Defence Equipment & Support published a UK5 transparency notice for Ajax in-service support.
Because GDUK is the original equipment manufacturer and design authority, the Department proposed a direct award under the Procurement Act 2023. The published lot is five years plus nine months, with two one-year options, advertised at £833.3 million excluding VAT and £1 billion including VAT, from July 2027, with service delivery from January 2028.
The scope includes fleet optimisation, update and upgrade tasking, design-authority services, safety-case support, configuration management, maintenance, repairs and training. Additional direct awards — including connectivity assurance testing for Bowman fit on Ajax — continue to flow to the same supplier. Manufacture payments have not stopped.

Set that against the Reserve. In September 2026 (this month) commanding officers were briefed to find in-year savings by reducing Army Reserve service by an estimated four to six days per reservist for the remainder of the year. Annual camp had already taken the bulk of days. The Regular Army, at around 70,000, cannot cover the demand.
A programme that has not delivered a safely usable fleet is being prepared for a billion-pound-class support settlement. The part-time force that would have to fight if the deterrent failed is being told to train less.
