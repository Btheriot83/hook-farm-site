## Transcript (~30s)

She said the last thing — and the one most riders never think about — is that Uber and Lyft are not transportation companies. They're pricing algorithms with cars attached.

"A taxi has a meter. The meter runs at a fixed rate per mile and per minute. The price is predictable. Rideshare doesn't work that way. The price changes every 30 seconds based on demand, supply, your location, the time of day, your ride history, whether you're a subscriber, and which zone your GPS is in. Two people standing on the same block going to the same place can pay $8 apart depending on which app they're using, whether they're in a surge zone, whether they scheduled in advance, and whether they waited 10 minutes for the Saver option."

She said the system is designed so the default choice is always the most expensive one.

"The default is the first app you open — without checking the other one. The default is your GPS location — without walking 2 blocks. The default is real-time pricing — without scheduling ahead. The default is UberX standard — without scrolling to Saver. The default is no subscription — without checking the math. Every default in the rideshare system is optimized for the platform's revenue, not your wallet."

She said the 10 things in this thread flip every default.

"Check both apps. Walk out of the surge zone. Schedule ahead. Use Wait and Save. Subscribe if you ride frequently. Set your pickup to a clean intersection. Maintain your rating. Avoid airport terminal pickups when possible. Tip $2–$3. And understand that the number on the screen is not the price you have to pay — it's the price you pay when you don't check."

She said over 4 years and 11,000 trips on the driver side, and thousands of rides as a passenger, the difference between a rider who knows the system and one who doesn't is $1,500–$2,500 per year.

"Same cities. Same routes. Same destinations. Same cars. The only difference is whether you pressed 'confirm' in 2 seconds or spent 30 seconds checking the things the app hopes you'll never check."
