## Transcript (~30s)

She said the sixth thing most riders don't know is that Uber and Lyft both offer subscription plans — and for frequent riders, they pay for themselves immediately.

Uber One costs roughly $9.99/month. Lyft Pink costs roughly $9.99/month. Most riders have never heard of either.

"Uber One gives you 6–8% off every eligible UberX ride, reduced surge pricing when surge is active, $0 delivery fee on Uber Eats orders, and 5% off Uber Eats orders over $15. If you take 4–5 Uber rides per month, the discount on rides alone covers the subscription cost. Everything after that is pure savings. If you also use Uber Eats, it pays for itself in the first week."

She said Lyft Pink offers similar benefits.

"Lyft Pink gives you a percentage discount on every ride, priority airport pickups, relaxed cancellation fees, and exclusive promotions. For riders who take 10+ Lyft rides per month, Pink saves $15–$25/month after the subscription cost. That's $180–$300/year in net savings."

She said most riders don't subscribe because they don't know these plans exist.

"Neither Uber nor Lyft promotes these subscriptions aggressively in the main ride-booking flow. They're buried in the profile or settings menu. Most riders have opened the app hundreds of times and never seen them. But the math is simple: if you ride 3+ times per week on either platform, the subscription saves you more than it costs. Every month. Without changing anything about how you ride."

She said she subscribes to both.

"I have Uber One and Lyft Pink simultaneously. Total cost: $20/month. I comparison shop every ride between both apps. Whichever is cheaper gets my trip — and I'm getting a subscriber discount on top of the lower fare. Over a month, the combined subscriptions save me $40–$60 net. That's $480–$720/year in savings for $240/year in subscriptions. The math works for anyone who rides 4+ times per week."
