## Transcript (~30s)

The trades jobs I worked involved generations of collected wisdom to do efficiently--in my case, working as a diesel mechanic.  

I wasn't taught in a trade school, I was taught by my great uncles, who had been doing it all their lives. I never bought a tool.  All my tools were gifted to me by those same great uncles. Any time I was stymied by some odd mechanical demon, they had the fix--lesson: there's ALWAYS a way. You just need to be creative. 

They even had something you'll never (or maybe only very rarely) see in a diesel mechanic's shop today--a full-blown machinists room. Lathes, everything (no, not CNC). And they knew how to use them, though most of them had at best a 6th grade formal education. 

White kids graduating from trade schools today are not likely to have that wealth of multi-generational hands-on wisdom in their trade of choice.  But whatever they have access to, however, they'll absorb like a sponge.

Third-world migrants from populations with average IQs in the 70s and 60s? No chance to ever be a master of their trade. They're just incapable.
