🇺🇸🇲🇽🇨🇦 Breaking: Mexico Says USMCA Trade Deal Will Continue

Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum stated that the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA) will remain in force despite recent comments from U.S. President Donald Trump suggesting he is “not looking to renew” the pact.  

What happened?

President Trump recently said the U.S. may not renew USMCA, arguing that the agreement has not benefited the United States enough and pointing to large trade deficits with Canada and Mexico.  
In response, President Sheinbaum emphasized that:
The agreement is vital for North American competitiveness.
Mexico expects the trade pact to continue.
Negotiations between the three countries are ongoing rather than ending.  

What is USMCA?

USMCA (called CUSMA in Canada and T-MEC in Mexico) replaced NAFTA in 2020 and governs one of the world’s largest free-trade regions. It covers:

🚗 Automotive manufacturing
🌾 Agriculture
💻 Digital trade
🏭 Manufacturing supply chains
📦 Cross-border commerce

The agreement supports approximately $1.6–2 trillion in annual trade among the three countries.  

Why is this important for Canada?

For Canada, maintaining USMCA means:

✅ Automotive sector stability

Canadian auto manufacturers rely on integrated supply chains that move parts across the U.S., Canada, and Mexico multiple times before final assembly.

✅ Energy exports

Canada remains one of the largest suppliers of oil, natural gas, and electricity to the United States.

✅ Agriculture

Farmers benefit from tariff-free access for many products, supporting billions of dollars in exports.

✅ Business certainty

A continuation of USMCA reduces uncertainty for companies making long-term investment decisions.

What happens next?

The agreement is entering its scheduled review process.
The three governments can:
Extend the agreement for another 16 years,
Renegotiate specific provisions, or
Allow it to enter annual review periods rather than ending immediately.  

Market implications

Positive if USMCA continues:

📈 Stable North American supply chains
📈 Lower tariff risk
📈 Better outlook for automakers, industrials, railroads, and logistics companies
📈 Improved investor confidence in Canada and Mexico

Risks if negotiations deteriorate:

⚠️ Higher tariffs
⚠️ Increased costs for manufacturers and consumers
⚠️ Greater volatility for CAD and MXN currencies
⚠️ Potential disruptions to automotive and agricultural exports
