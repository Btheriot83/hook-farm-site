## Transcript (~30s)

She said the ninth thing most riders don't do is tip — and she said it changes the driver experience more than passengers realize.

She said tipping isn't just about generosity. It's strategic.

"Uber and Lyft show drivers your tip history in subtle ways. On Uber, drivers can see if you tipped on previous rides when they're deciding whether to accept your request. A rider who tips consistently gets prioritized by the algorithm and by individual drivers who recognize them. A rider who never tips gets the leftover drivers — the ones who are new, low-rated, or desperate enough to take any ride."

She said the tip doesn't have to be large.

"A $2–$3 tip on a $15 ride changes the driver's perception completely. It takes your ride from unprofitable to worthwhile. Most riders don't realize that after Uber and Lyft take their cut — which is 25–40% of the fare — the driver earns $8–$10 on a $15 ride. After gas, insurance, and vehicle depreciation, the net is $4–$6. A $2 tip increases the driver's take-home by 30–40%. It's significant."

She said from the driver's side, the worst rides are riders who make you wait 3 minutes at pickup, give you a wrong address, slam the door, leave garbage, and don't tip.

"Those rides lose money. The driver spent 8 minutes driving to you, 3 minutes waiting, 15 minutes on the trip, and the driver nets $4 after expenses. They would have earned more sitting in a parking lot waiting for a better request. When that same rider requests again next week, the driver sees the history and declines. The rider waits longer for a driver. The longer wait sometimes means surge pricing kicks in. No tip leads to worse service leads to higher costs. It compounds."

She said tipping in cash is even more impactful than tipping in the app.

"A cash tip at the end of the ride is immediate and visible. The driver sees it, appreciates it, and associates you with a positive experience. An in-app tip arrives later — sometimes hours after the ride — and it's less emotionally impactful. Both are good. Cash is better. Either way, $2–$3 on every ride transforms your rideshare experience over time."
