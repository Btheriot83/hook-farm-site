## Transcript (~30s)

Building a million dollar agency from Sri Lanka. Nobody saw that coming. Not even me.

It started simple. I knew design. Thimasha knew design. We had two laptops and a problem we genuinely wanted to solve. Founders were spending a fortune on creative and getting chaos in return. We figured we could fix that.

So we built an offer around it and started knocking on doors.

For three months nothing happened.

Cold outreach. Ads. Platforms. Product Hunt. Every single thing we could think of. Zero clients. Zero revenue. Just two people in a room refusing to quit.

Then one night I was dialing leads at midnight and a lady picked up. I figured out she was Sri Lankan. Asked her. She laughed and said yes. We jumped on a call.

That was Upeka from Diana, YC W24. Our first client. She did not just give us the work. She introduced us to a world we did not know existed. The SF startup ecosystem. YC founders. Everything Zyner is today traces back to that one phone call.

From there we learned everything the hard way.

Lost clients. Delivered work that was not good enough and had to own it. Figured out that great design with bad project management is useless. That great project management with no creative direction is equally useless. That all of it means nothing if the client does not feel like they are being taken care of.

We kept going anyway.

Team went from 5 to 80. Clients went from 1 to 320+. Revenue crossed $1M ARR.

Here is what actually made the difference.

We hired slowly. Every single person trained properly before touching a client file. No shortcuts on that. Ever.

We built SOPs before we needed them. Every scenario documented. Everyone knows exactly what to do when things go wrong. And things always go wrong at some point.

We stayed close to clients. I still reply on every Slack thread personally. Not because I have to. Because I want to. The relationship matters as much as the work.

We charged what the work was worth. The moment we stopped being scared about pricing everything changed. Better clients. Better projects. Better outcomes.

And we never stopped showing up online. Posts. Case studies. Sharing the story. Inbound does not happen in a week. It happens after months of consistency that most people give up on too early.

That is the whole thing honestly.

No secret. No shortcut. Just showing up every single day and refusing to quit on something worth building.

If you are building a service business right now and wondering if it is possible. It is. I promise you it is.

What is the hardest part of your journey right now? Drop it below.
